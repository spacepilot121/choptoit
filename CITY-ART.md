# City artwork

The backgrounds are original polygon illustrations inspired by recognisable buildings, rather than accurate reconstructions or traced photographs. Perspective is compressed to preserve the portrait game's sky and target lanes. The skyline uses medieval and early Tudor landmarks; Oxford uses Magdalen's tower rather than the much later Radcliffe Camera.

`scripts/town-landmarks.cjs` defines the silhouettes. `scripts/angular-art.cjs --towns-only` regenerates the fifteen editable SVGs and runtime PNGs. Animated market stalls, sellers, lanterns, smoke, flags and ships are in `cast-art.js`. Chimney smoke uses the same roof anchors as the generated houses. Foreground shoes meet the street at y=1050; the chopping deck begins at y=1080.

Pole targets use the holder's world transform to follow the actual rotated pole tip. The same result positions their collision bodies. Collected targets release the attachment for the catch-and-exit animation.

Scenery review: detached York ledges and Chester railings were replaced with complete timber buildings. Bridge arches now have genuine open spans. Shutters frame the glass, tower ornament stays within the belfry, and flagpoles meet foreground roof ridges. York's small castle silhouette now suggests Clifford's lobed tower. Stone facets, door recesses, steps and planted pots add detail without covering the target lanes.

Travel uses five original side-on landscapes generated by `scripts/angular-art.cjs --roads-only`: Hull's flat estuary and reed beds; coastal routes to Dover and Southampton; uplands to Durham, Newcastle and Lincoln; wooded routes to Canterbury, Oxford and Gloucester; farmland for the remaining destinations. These are stylised route themes, not exact geographic itineraries. All share a horizontal dirt road, wheel ruts and roadside fences. Passing vegetation and signs have separate foreground/background speeds. Runtime windmills, waves, chimney smoke, grazing sheep, travellers, polygon deer and birds provide movement. Each caravan upgrade remains visible during travel.

Hull's nave meets a continuous stone quay, rather than disappearing into a water overlay. Its pale dressed tower and brick transepts are informed by the [Hull Old Town appraisal](https://www.hull.gov.uk/downloads/file/3198/OldTownAppraisal.pdf). Foreground boats fit the narrow channel. Layered clustered tree canopies now share the same geometry in generated landscapes and moving roadside trees; branches and leaves stay connected.

The detail pass adds facial planes and hair strands, costume seams and armour facets, clipped blade engravings, recessed doors and lanterns, richer targets and basket weave, embossed coins, chest ironwork and plank platforms. Atlas frame dimensions, expression indices and weapon cutting edges are retained.

`caravan-art.js` supplies both workshop previews and travel geometry. Four articulated animal legs move in alternating phases; torso motion is separate, and all standing feet share the wheel ground line. Every level adds width/cargo, with complete covers at level 6, guild colours, lanterns and heraldry at level 9, and pennants and gold trim at level 12. Gameplay capacity and upgrade prices are unchanged. Real Phaser object tests cover all 16 caravan levels, five scenery families, six target art types, every city, and animation cleanup.
